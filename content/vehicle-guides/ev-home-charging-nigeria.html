Every EV supplied by Sure Imports comes with a compatible wall-mountable charger, sometimes called a charging pile. The supplied charger works with standard Nigerian mains electricity. You can therefore plan to charge at home or at your business premises instead of making public charging your everyday starting point.

The useful question is how charging will fit your parking space, electrical installation and daily schedule. This guide explains what is included, what to ask your installer and what changes if you want to use another charger.

What comes with your vehicle?

Your EV includes its matched wall-mountable charger. You do not need to shop for a separate charger simply to start using the vehicle. Installation work is a separate consideration: your quotation should identify any installation services included, while the charger documentation supplies the electrical requirements.

Request the charger data sheet before scheduling installation. Record the rated charging power, installation instructions, cable arrangement and any operating controls. Do not infer a charger’s power from the vehicle’s battery capacity: a battery labelled in kWh does not tell you the charger’s output in kW.

Standard mains compatibility and your actual installation

Compatibility with Nigerian mains electricity means the supplied equipment is intended to work with that supply. The installer still needs to check the circuit serving the parking area, other electrical loads and the proposed charger location. A suitable supply at the building does not make every existing socket or extension lead suitable for sustained charging.

The US Department of Energy’s home-charging guidance identifies electrical capacity, equipment location and qualified installation as practical considerations. Its US voltage categories and installation rules are not Nigerian specifications; use the supplied charger manual and an installer familiar with local requirements.

A useful brief for your installer

  • Parking: show where the vehicle will normally stop and which side its charging port is on. Check that the cable can reach without crossing a pedestrian route.
  • Equipment: provide the actual charger manual and rating, not only a vehicle brochure.
  • Electrical loads: describe air conditioning, pumps, refrigeration or machinery that may run during charging.
  • Location: discuss weather exposure, physical protection, access and the manufacturer’s mounting requirements.
  • Responsibility: agree who supplies installation materials, commissions the equipment and explains its controls.
  • Records: ask for the installation and commissioning records and keep them with the vehicle documents.

Ask for a written scope and itemised price. That makes it easier to compare proposals and distinguish necessary work from optional conveniences. If you rent the premises or share parking, obtain the relevant owner or management agreement before work begins.

Build charging around the time you are parked

Write down when the vehicle normally returns and when it next leaves. A delivery van parked for ten hours has a different charging opportunity from a passenger vehicle that returns between two short shifts. Consider interruptions too: an eight-hour overnight window is not necessarily eight hours of available electricity.

Start with the energy used on a normal working day. You are usually replacing that day’s consumption, not filling an empty battery every night. Our charging-time guide shows how to calculate the required charging window without treating a theoretical figure as a guarantee.

Can you use a public charger or another wall charger?

The included charger is matched to your vehicle. For another charger, check the exact connector and charging standard, whether the session is AC or DC, the vehicle’s supported charging capability and the operator’s access requirements. A physically similar plug is not sufficient evidence that a session will work.

Before planning a journey around a public site, ask the operator to confirm compatibility, current availability, access hours and payment arrangements. Do not buy an adapter based only on its appearance. Obtain confirmation that it is approved for the intended vehicle and charging equipment.

Before your first charging session

Ask the installer to demonstrate starting and stopping a session, reading normal status indications and responding to a fault. Follow the manual’s sequence for connection and disconnection. Keep the charging area clear, and report damaged equipment or abnormal behaviour rather than repeatedly resetting it.

For your first week, record charging start and finish times, electricity used where metering is available and departure battery levels. These simple records will make your next decision—whether to change the schedule or investigate an installation issue—much more informed.
