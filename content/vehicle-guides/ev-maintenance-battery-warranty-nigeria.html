Buying an EV for Nigeria means planning how it will be maintained, who will diagnose a fault and how parts will reach you. Lower routine maintenance can be an advantage, but it does not replace a service plan or a written warranty.

This guide is a buying and ownership checklist. Warranty periods, mileage limits, local parts stocks and repair prices must be confirmed for your exact Sure Imports configuration; we do not substitute another manufacturer’s terms for those of your vehicle.

Which maintenance tasks change?

A battery-electric vehicle removes combustion-engine tasks such as engine-oil changes and spark-plug replacement. It still has tyres, brakes, suspension, steering and cabin equipment. Depending on the model, cooling systems and other fluids can also need attention. Follow its service schedule rather than assuming “electric” means “maintenance-free”.

The Department of Energy’s maintenance overview explains why all-electric vehicles generally need less routine maintenance and why battery and cooling requirements remain model-specific. The guide is evidence for the distinction, not a Nigerian servicing-price list.

Request the service schedule before payment

Ask for intervals by time and distance, the work due at each interval, approved consumables and the records needed to preserve warranty cover. For commercial use, describe the actual operating pattern so the supplier can identify any relevant severe-duty requirements.

Have the service provider quote against the schedule. “Annual service” is too vague if one quote includes inspections only and another includes parts and fluids. Record VAT, labour, diagnostic charges and travel or collection costs separately where applicable.

Separate three different battery questions

  1. Daily charging: what charge limits and storage practice does the manual recommend for this battery and software?
  2. Condition: how is battery health assessed, and which report or diagnostic reading is available?
  3. Warranty: what defects or capacity loss are covered, over what time and distance, and under which exclusions?

Do not apply a universal “always charge to 80%” rule to every chemistry or configuration. Likewise, a range change on one difficult trip is not by itself a diagnosis of permanent battery degradation. Compare similar conditions and use the manufacturer’s diagnostic process when investigating a concern.

Put the warranty questions in writing

  • Who is the party responsible for handling a claim in Nigeria?
  • Which terms apply separately to the battery, drivetrain, charger and rest of the vehicle?
  • Is commercial use covered, and are there mileage or operating restrictions?
  • What service records and diagnostic evidence are required?
  • Who pays for diagnosis, labour, shipping and vehicle transport?
  • What happens if a covered component is unavailable locally?
  • How are exclusions and disagreements handled under the agreed terms?

A written answer is more useful than a long headline warranty with no defined claim process. Keep the final terms, configuration, invoice and handover records together.

Build a parts basket for the intended job

For a business vehicle, obtain availability and quotes for likely consumables and repair items: the specified tyres, brake components, filters where fitted, suspension wear items, lights and body parts. Ask separately about charging cables, onboard charging equipment and major electrical components.

Identify part numbers and compatibility with the exact configuration. A shared model name is not enough to establish that a part fits every production version. Ask whether the quoted price is ex-China, landed in Nigeria or fitted locally, and what lead time it assumes.

Budget downtime without inventing failure rates

Keep planned service downtime separate from unexpected repairs. For a delivery business, an unavailable vehicle may require a rental or cause lost contribution from missed work. For a private owner, the cost may be alternative transport. Use the consequence relevant to your operation.

Do not assume that every vehicle will need a full battery replacement during a five-year budget, or that none could need a major repair. Show a base case using the service schedule and separate repair scenarios using actual quotes when available. Our total-cost comparison explains how to combine these with energy costs.

A useful handover record

Check the vehicle identification, selected configuration, visible condition, keys, included wall-mountable charger and agreed documents. Ask for a demonstration of normal charging indications and the procedure for reporting a fault. Record unresolved discrepancies promptly with photographs and your order reference.

During ownership, maintain a dated service and charging log. Keep receipts, fault descriptions and repair reports. Do not open high-voltage equipment yourself; qualified personnel should handle diagnosis and repair under the manufacturer’s procedure.
