A fleet charging plan should answer one practical question: will every vehicle have enough energy for its next assigned job? Counting the chargers supplied with the vehicles is only the beginning. You also need a timetable, electrical capacity and someone responsible for checking that each session actually happens.

Each Sure Imports EV includes a wall-mountable charger compatible with standard Nigerian mains electricity. For several vehicles at one site, assess the combined load and the operation of the whole depot before installation.

Make a route-and-energy register

Create one line for each planned vehicle: configuration, payload or passengers, daily kilometres, departure time, return time and energy required. Include refrigerated or other powered equipment in the operating description. Record the busiest realistic day as well as an average day.

For a starting illustration, three vehicles each needing 24 kWh back in the battery require 72 kWh in total. At an assumed 90% charging efficiency, that is 80 kWh at the charging supply. These are planning assumptions, not performance figures for a named vehicle.

Check both the energy window and peak demand

Suppose each charger draws an assumed 7 kW and each vehicle needs 26.67 kWh from the supply. Each session takes about 3.81 hours at that sustained rate. Simultaneous charging asks for 21 kW before other depot loads; fully sequential charging takes about 11.43 hours. An eight-hour window cannot accommodate the fully sequential schedule at those assumptions.

That does not automatically mean you need a larger electrical connection. A designer could evaluate overlapping sessions, a different charging schedule or validated load management. The right answer depends on the actual chargers, vehicle limits and site. Do not assume the included units support remote control or automatic power sharing unless confirmed.

The Department of Energy’s infrastructure planning guidance treats travel patterns, charging needs and site development as linked decisions. Use that planning approach with Nigerian site requirements, rather than copying foreign electrical specifications.

Price the depot’s electricity with a documented sample

NERC’s September 2026 IE Ogun end-user tariff table lists A–Non-MD at ₦209.50/kWh. At that published sample rate, the illustrative 80 kWh daily charging requirement has an energy component of ₦16,760 per day, or ₦435,760 over 26 days, before separately applied taxes or other charges.

Use the tariff class actually applicable to your depot; a maximum-demand account may have a different classification. Add backup energy at its own calculated cost. Design the parking operation as well as the wiring

  • Assign charging bays and check that the correct vehicle can reach its connector.
  • Keep delivery movements and pedestrians clear of charging cables.
  • Define who plugs in, checks the charging status and reports a failed session.
  • Record departure charge requirements by route rather than using one unexplained target for every vehicle.
  • Plan what happens if one charger, bay or supply source is unavailable.
  • Keep site and equipment documentation accessible to authorised maintenance staff.

Where more than one shift uses the vehicles, make charging part of the handover. A driver saying “I plugged it in” is not the same as confirming that the session started and delivered the required energy.

Pilot before you scale

Use an initial group of vehicles to measure the operation. Track kilometres, loads, metered charging energy, departures missed, backup use and service events. Compare vehicles doing similar work, and investigate outliers rather than averaging them away.

A successful pilot should show that the route can be completed with the chosen reserve and that the next departure is sustainable. It should also identify who resolves charging faults, how spare parts are obtained and how replacement transport is arranged.

What to send with a fleet enquiry

Provide model or body requirements, quantities, payload, route diary, operating hours, depot location and the available electricity arrangement. Identify any deadline that depends on a contract or seasonal work. Ask for confirmed vehicle configuration, charger ratings, supply timing and support terms in the quotation.
