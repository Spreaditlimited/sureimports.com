The short answer is that there is no single CCTV camera price in Nigeria. A product page may show the price of one camera, a recorder-only device, a boxed kit or a solar camera, while an installed quotation may also include storage, cabling, power backup, configuration and labour. On 9 October 2026, the Nigerian listings checked for this guide showed ₦75,000 for a dated 6MP 4G solar camera offer, ₦120,000 for a four-lens solar 4G marketplace listing and ₦185,000 for an eight-camera DVR kit. Those prices are reference listings, not a calculated market range or proof of product authenticity.

Before comparing prices, make every seller state what is inside the box, what remains to be purchased and whether installation is included. Resolution labels, lens counts and words such as “original,” “4K” or “24/7” should be treated as seller claims until the exact model and performance are verified.

Camera-only price versus complete installed-system price

A camera-only price normally covers the camera and whatever accessories are explicitly listed. A complete system may require cameras, DVR or NVR, surveillance hard drive, power supplies or PoE switching, cable, connectors, network equipment, mounting boxes, conduit or trunking, surge protection, backup power, display equipment, configuration and labour.

Even a product described as a “complete kit” may omit the hard drive, monitor, router, data plan, cable length or installation. Ask the seller to mark every line as included, optional or excluded. For an installed quotation, also define drilling, work at height, cable routing, wall repairs, travel, user training, account handover and post-installation support.

Do not compare a ₦120,000 solar camera listing with a ₦185,000 eight-camera kit by price alone. The useful comparison is cost per required viewpoint after storage, connectivity, power, installation and support have been made equivalent.

An XVR may accept several analogue formats and sometimes a limited number of IP cameras, but the exact channel mix and supported resolutions must be confirmed from its model-specific specification. Likewise, an ONVIF label does not guarantee that every feature, such as audio, analytics or PTZ control, will work across different brands.

For a repeatable installation, record the exact camera, recorder and firmware combination that passed testing. Do not substitute a cheaper camera or recorder simply because the connector looks the same.

Lens choice, identification and night sample footage

Megapixels do not tell you whether a face or vehicle plate will be identifiable at the required distance. The image must place enough pixels across the target. A wider lens covers more area but spreads the available pixels across that area; a narrower lens sees less of the scene but gives more detail within its chosen zone.

For example, a 1920-pixel-wide image covering eight metres provides about 240 pixels per metre. The same image covering four metres provides about 480 pixels per metre. A Hikvision datasheet explaining DORI uses 250 pixels per metre as its identification reference. DORI distances are planning indicators, not a promise that every face will be usable under poor lighting, motion or compression.

Request original day and night clips from the proposed lens, resolution and mounting height. The sample should include a person walking toward, across and away from the camera at the actual identification distance. Test infrared reflections from walls, insects, rain, number plates and light-coloured clothing. Also test backlight from gates, windows and vehicle headlights.

Ask for exported recorder footage rather than a video filmed from a showroom monitor. Exported footage reveals the recorded bitrate, frame rate, compression, timestamp and playback quality that the customer will actually receive.

Recorder channels, codecs, bandwidth and PoE budgets

An eight-channel recorder is not automatically suitable for any eight cameras. Confirm the maximum supported resolution per channel, incoming recording bandwidth, playback capacity, supported codecs, audio channels, alarm inputs, hard-drive bays and maximum disk size.

Codec support must match from camera to recorder. H.265 can reduce storage compared with H.264 under suitable conditions, but the actual result depends on movement, noise, frame rate, image complexity and the manufacturer’s implementation. If a camera advertises H.265+ or another enhanced codec, confirm that the recorder can record and export that mode without disabling required features.

For PoE, compare the maximum camera load with both the per-port limit and the switch’s total power budget. Eight cameras rated at a maximum of 12W each could demand 96W when infrared LEDs, white lights, audio or PTZ functions are active. An eight-port switch with an 80W total budget would therefore be undersized even though it has enough physical ports. Keep a documented power margin rather than designing exactly to the advertised limit.

Storage worked example with explicit assumptions

Assume eight cameras, an average recorded bitrate of 2 megabits per second per camera, continuous recording for 24 hours a day and a 30-day retention target. Assume the quoted bitrate already includes the selected resolution, codec, frame rate and scene conditions.

  1. Total bitrate: 8 cameras × 2Mbps = 16Mbps.
  2. Convert bits to bytes: 16Mbps ÷ 8 = 2 megabytes per second.
  3. Daily storage: 2MB × 86,400 seconds = 172,800MB, or about 172.8GB per day using decimal units.
  4. Thirty days: 172.8GB × 30 = 5.184TB of raw video.
  5. Add a 15% planning allowance for variation and operating overhead: approximately 5.96TB.

This example points to at least 6TB of usable recording capacity, not merely a drive labelled 6TB. Formatting, recorder reservation, variable bitrate, audio and real scene activity can change retention.

If motion recording produces a genuine 25% recording duty cycle, the same assumptions give about 1.296TB raw for 30 days, or roughly 1.49TB after the same allowance. Do not sell that as guaranteed retention: a busy shop, roadside camera, moving trees or insects can keep motion recording active far longer than expected.

A 4G camera is not “free to run” because it has a solar panel. Live viewing, notifications, firmware downloads and cloud recording can consume mobile data. Ask the supplier for measured data use under event recording, frequent live viewing and continuous upload. Confirm whether local SD-card recording continues when mobile service is unavailable.

Solar CCTV sizing and recording duty cycle

A solar camera must survive the night and low-sun periods, not just operate during a sunny demonstration. Battery capacity stated only in milliamp-hours is incomplete unless the battery voltage is also given. For example, 10,800mAh does not by itself reveal watt-hours, which is the useful energy figure.

Consider a hypothetical camera averaging 6W during continuous operation. It uses 144Wh per day. With four equivalent peak-sun hours and 75% overall charging efficiency, the theoretical panel requirement is 144Wh ÷ 4 ÷ 0.75 = 48W. Real design must also account for cloudy days, heat, panel angle, dirt, battery ageing and periods when infrared LEDs, floodlights or 4G transmission increase consumption.

For 24 hours of battery autonomy at 6W with 80% usable battery capacity, the nominal battery requirement is 6W × 24 ÷ 0.8 = 180Wh. A camera designed mainly for PIR-triggered events may average much less, but it may miss activity before wake-up or provide gaps between events. “Always-on video” can also mean low-frame-rate standby rather than full-rate continuous recording, so request a written explanation.

When a recorder and several cameras need a separate backup supply, use the runtime method in our portable solar-generator buying guide. Include the recorder, network equipment and camera load in the calculation; a battery that keeps a camera on while the router loses power will not preserve remote viewing.

Cybersecurity configuration and support

A connected security camera is also a network device. Change all default passwords, give every site unique credentials and enable multifactor authentication where the platform supports it. Use separate administrator and viewing accounts, remove unused users and transfer recovery email addresses and phone numbers to the customer at handover.

Update firmware from the identified manufacturer or authorised support channel. Disable services that are not required, including unnecessary peer-to-peer access, UPnP, exposed web administration or unused audio. Where practical, isolate CCTV equipment from business computers and payment systems using a separate network or VLAN.

Record the app name, publisher, account owner, firmware version, local recording behaviour and subscription dependencies. The support plan should explain who resets accounts, updates firmware, replaces failed disks, checks camera time and exports evidence. A cheap camera tied to an abandoned app can become more expensive than a supported product.

Sample tests before a bulk order or installation

Before paying for samples, follow the supplier verification checklist. Record the sample evidence in a supplier scorecard so the lowest price does not outweigh missing technical support.

  1. Confirm the exact model numbers on the camera, carton, interface and specification sheet.
  2. Record day, dusk and full-night footage at the required identification distance.
  3. Test moving faces, clothing detail, vehicle motion and backlit entrances.
  4. Fill all recorder channels and verify recording, playback and simultaneous remote viewing.
  5. Run PoE cameras with infrared or white lights active to test the maximum power condition.
  6. Disconnect internet service and confirm what still records locally.
  7. Interrupt mains power and measure restart behaviour, retained settings and actual backup time.
  8. Fill or simulate a full storage cycle and confirm overwrite behaviour and retention.
  9. Export footage to a normal computer and verify that video, audio and timestamps play correctly.
  10. For solar units, test several nights, cloudy charging, alerts, event wake-up and mobile data use.

Keep the original sample, test footage, firmware file and approved bill of materials as the reference for production inspection. A supplier should not replace the sensor, lens, battery cell, solar panel, chipset, app or accessories without written approval.

Decision for a single local buyer and for an importer

Single home or small-business buyer

Start with a site survey and an installer who can demonstrate identification footage at your required distances. Buy the supported outcome rather than the highest lens count. Require an itemised quote, recorder access, administrator credentials, storage calculation, data-cost explanation and handover test. Importing one unfamiliar camera may save little after shipping, compatibility work and local troubleshooting.

Importer, distributor or repeat installer

Define a controlled product line instead of collecting unrelated marketplace links. Specify camera models, lens options, recorder combinations, storage assumptions, plug and power requirements, app ownership, firmware support, packaging, spare units and inspection tests. Compare landed cost only after the bill of materials is fixed.

Frequently asked questions

What is the price of a CCTV camera in Nigeria?

It depends on whether the offer is one camera, a solar camera, a recorder kit or an installed system. The checked examples in this article were ₦75,000, ₦120,000 and ₦185,000, with different inclusions and stock conditions. They are not a market range.

Does a CCTV kit include a hard drive?

Not necessarily. If the listing does not name the drive capacity and model, treat storage as unconfirmed. Ask for usable capacity and a retention calculation based on camera bitrate and recording schedule.

Is a solar CCTV camera suitable for continuous recording?

Only if its average load, battery, panel and low-sun reserve support continuous operation. Many battery cameras are designed around event recording or reduced-frame-rate standby. Verify the recording mode with a multi-day sample test.

Is Wi-Fi CCTV cheaper than wired CCTV?

It may reduce data cabling, but it can require stronger routers, repeaters and backup power. Wired equipment may cost more to install initially but provide a more predictable local connection. Compare the full installed design.

How much storage do eight cameras need?

Using the explicit example above, eight cameras averaging 2Mbps each require about 5.184TB raw for 30 days of continuous recording, or approximately 5.96TB after a 15% planning allowance. Different bitrates and recording duty cycles produce different results.

What should I verify before paying for a solar 4G camera?

Verify model, lens views, recorded resolution, battery watt-hours, panel watts, event or continuous mode, supported networks, local storage, data use, app publisher, night footage, included SIM or memory card and what happens when mobile service fails.
