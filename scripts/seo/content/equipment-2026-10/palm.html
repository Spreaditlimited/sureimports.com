Buying a palm oil processing machine in Nigeria starts with a process question, not a price question: what material will enter the equipment, and what finished product must leave it? A machine advertised as a palm oil press may be only one station in a mill. Another supplier may use the same label for an integrated package. Unless the feed state, process stages and capacity basis are written into the quotation, two apparently similar offers may be completely different.

This guide covers equipment for producing mesocarp palm oil from fresh palm fruit bunches. It uses the current Nigerian Institute for Oil Palm Research equipment information and the FAO's small-scale palm oil processing reference as process sources. The FAO publication dates from 2002: it remains useful for understanding unit operations, but its historical capacities, costs and manufacturer lists are not current quotations, performance guarantees or vendor endorsements. Information and public listings were checked on 9 October 2026.

Palm fruit oil and palm kernel oil are different projects

The red palm oil commonly associated with fresh-fruit processing comes from the fleshy mesocarp surrounding the palm nut. Palm kernel oil comes from the kernel inside that nut. The feed preparation, pressing equipment and downstream handling are different.

Project Starting material Main preparation Press feed
Mesocarp palm oil Fresh fruit bunches or loose palm fruits Bunch handling, sterilisation or cooking, stripping where required, and digestion Digested palm fruit mash containing mesocarp, fibre and nuts
Palm kernel oil Recovered palm nuts or separated kernels Fibre and nut separation, nut conditioning, cracking, shell separation and kernel preparation Prepared palm kernels

A general oilseed expeller or palm kernel press is therefore not a complete fresh-fruit-bunch line. It cannot receive harvested bunches and independently perform bunch sterilisation, fruit removal, digestion, mesocarp pressing, clarification and drying. Likewise, a palm fruit screw press is not automatically suitable for prepared kernels.

Ask every seller to name the exact feed accepted at the inlet. Terms such as palm oil machine, expeller, digester press and complete mill are not sufficiently precise on their own. Request photographs and video showing the stated feed entering the offered model, rather than images of an unrelated factory.

What a complete palm fruit oil line should cover

A complete line is a connected process with defined handovers between stages. The exact arrangement can differ: some small systems strip bunches before cooking loose fruit, while other configurations sterilise whole bunches before mechanical stripping. The buyer should not combine units designed around incompatible sequences.

Stage Purpose What to confirm in the offer
Bunch reception and handling Receive, sort, weigh where required and move incoming FFB Receiving platform, loading method, conveyors or manual handling limits, drainage and rejected-fruit arrangements
Sterilisation or cooking Condition the fruit for separation, digestion and pressing Whole-bunch or loose-fruit feed, batch size, thermal source, pressure status, loading method and complete cycle basis
Stripping or threshing Separate fruits from bunch stalks Whether it works before or after sterilisation, FFB capacity, fruit carry-over and empty-bunch discharge
Digestion Break down conditioned mesocarp before pressing Batch or continuous operation, working volume, drive power, discharge control and compatible press feed rate
Pressing Apply mechanical pressure to digested fruit mash Fruit-mash input, press type, drive, wear parts, adjustment method and fibre-and-nut discharge
Clarification and drying Separate oil from water, fibre and non-oily solids, then reduce residual moisture for storage Screens, tanks, pumps, filters, separators or dryers actually included; never assume a press alone produces storage-ready oil
Oil storage Hold processed oil before transfer or sale Tank material, usable volume, outlet arrangement, cleaning access and any heating system
Kernel recovery Recover nuts and separate kernels for sale or later processing Fibre-nut separation, nut handling, cracker and kernel-shell separator; kernel oil extraction should be quoted as a separate process line

NIFOR's current FAQ illustrates why buyers must request a scope list. It describes its larger and medium small-scale equipment sets as including sterilisation, a digester-screw press, clarification and fruit screening, while its mini set is described more narrowly as a cooker, digester and press. A set name does not remove the need to identify what happens before the first supplied machine and after the last one.

Standalone press versus an integrated line

A standalone palm fruit press may make sense when an existing processor already has compatible cooking, digestion, clarification and material-handling equipment. It may also replace a worn press in an operating mill. For a new factory receiving FFB, however, purchasing only the press leaves major process gaps.

Before accepting a standalone offer, document who supplies the digested mash, how quickly it reaches the press, where the expressed liquid goes, how fibre and nuts are removed, and how the oil will be clarified, dried and stored. Include the required pumps, screens, pipework, chutes, platforms and controls. These connecting items are often absent from a headline machine description but determine whether the factory can run as one system.

Capacity must state the feed and the time basis

Capacity labels are commonly expressed in three incompatible ways:

  • FFB per hour: the mass of fresh fruit bunches accepted by the line.
  • Loose fruit per hour: fruit already removed from the bunch, which excludes stalk material and may also assume prior conditioning.
  • Oil per hour or per day: claimed output that depends on fruit condition, process configuration, operating discipline and the supplier's calculation basis.

A 1 tonne-per-hour loose-fruit press is not automatically a 1 tonne-per-hour FFB line. Nor should oil output be converted into FFB capacity using an assumed extraction percentage. Ask the supplier to provide a mass-flow sheet identifying the material entering every station and the calculation used to match the units.

A hypothetical bottleneck calculation

Assume, only for capacity comparison, that a quoted steriliser accepts 600 kilograms of FFB per batch and the supplier declares a complete 1.5-hour cycle including loading, processing, discharge and turnaround. Its average capacity is therefore 600 divided by 1.5, or 400 kilograms FFB per hour. Assume the thresher is rated at 800 kilograms FFB per hour, while the digester and press are each quoted at 500 kilograms of loose fruit per hour.

The steriliser appears to limit FFB flow to 400 kilograms per hour. However, the loose-fruit machines still cannot be declared compatible until the supplier demonstrates how much representative loose fruit reaches them from that FFB flow. These figures are hypothetical arithmetic, not operating instructions, extraction-rate promises or recommended machine sizes.

Capacity should be tested over a meaningful run that includes loading, transfers, cleaning interruptions and discharge. A press's instantaneous feed rate during a short demonstration is not the same as the sustained throughput of the entire line.

Batch, semi-continuous and continuous operation

Batch equipment processes a defined load and then stops or changes state for discharge and reloading. It can be practical at smaller scales, but the buyer must calculate average throughput from the full cycle rather than vessel capacity alone. Multiple batch vessels can overlap operations, provided labour, thermal supply and downstream equipment can support them.

Semi-continuous lines combine batch stages with continuously fed machines. For example, conditioned fruit may leave batches and feed a digester or press over time. Buffer capacity and operator coordination become important. A continuous line is designed for steadier material flow, usually with more conveyors, controls, pumps and supporting utilities. Greater automation can reduce manual transfers, but it also increases integration and maintenance requirements.

Ask for a cycle chart showing when each machine loads, runs, waits and discharges. This reveals queues that a simple list of nameplate capacities will not show.

Size the plant around dependable fruit supply

Start with the FFB that can reliably arrive during peak and low-supply periods, not the quantity a seller says a machine can process. Record the source of fruit, harvesting pattern, journey time, road access, vehicle payload arrangements, unloading hours and the condition in which bunches normally arrive.

Seasonality matters. A line selected only for the busiest weeks may sit underused for long periods, while an undersized plant may create queues and deterioration during peak harvest. Build monthly supply scenarios using records from your own plantation, committed growers or suppliers. Separate confirmed supply from optimistic future purchases.

Transport and handling can also change processing performance. Your request for quotation should describe whether fruit arrives as whole bunches, manually stripped fruit or a mixture; the expected cleanliness; the time between harvesting and processing; and whether the reception area must handle muddy or rain-wet loads. Do not ask the machinery supplier to guarantee oil recovery from an undefined feed.

Why this guide uses “quote required” instead of a price range

A cooker, digester-press and clarification package has a different scope from a standalone press or a line that also includes kernel recovery. Without a comparable equipment schedule, a price range hides the missing stages. Ask for current itemised quotations against the same requirements.

Every machine and complete line in this guide is therefore quote required. Obtain written offers for the same process boundary, feed state, capacity basis and test conditions before comparing cost. A transparent project budget should contain separate allowances for:

Local fabrication or imported equipment?

Option Potential advantages Points to verify
Local fabrication Easier physical inspection, possible access to fabricators, simpler modification and potentially faster supply of common parts Documented capacity basis, material grades, workmanship, repeatability, guarding, drawings, installed references and service response
Imported line Broader range of integrated layouts, automation levels and specialist components Supplier identity, factory capability, Nigerian site integration, spare-parts plan, documentation, local technical support and acceptance testing before dispatch

Neither origin guarantees quality. A strong local fabricator can be a better choice than a poorly supported imported package, while an experienced overseas manufacturer may offer integration that is difficult to obtain locally. Compare evidence and lifecycle support, not country labels. Buyers considering China can use the practical checks in Sourcing Machines from China to Nigeria.

Check the site before ordering the line

Request a utility schedule showing every motor, heater, pump and control load, including starting method and whether major motors can start together. Generator selection should be based on starting behaviour and the complete plant load, not the sum of motor nameplates alone. See the generator sizing guide for industrial machines for the questions an electrical professional should resolve.

  • Water: define the sources, storage, process use, cleaning demand and drainage route. Ask whether water quality affects any supplied component.
  • Thermal energy: identify whether the system uses steam, heated water or another engineered source. Boilers, pressure vessels, fuel systems and pressurised pipework require professional design, installation and commissioning. They are not DIY components.
  • Civil layout: confirm equipment weights, dynamic loads, anchor plans, access for maintenance, operator walkways, lifting space and future expansion.
  • Effluent and solids: map every discharge before construction. Wastewater, sludge, fibre, empty bunches, nuts and shells require planned collection and handling rather than uncontrolled release.
  • Cleaning: require safe access to product-contact areas, drainable low points and written cleaning procedures that do not expose staff to moving machinery or hot surfaces.
  • Guarding: rotating shafts, belts, chains, screws, couplings and hot surfaces should have suitable guards. Emergency stops and isolation points should be identified in the technical offer.

For questions about standards or conformity requirements for the exact machine being purchased, provide the product description, specification, end use and origin to the Standards Organisation of Nigeria enquiry centre. Applicability should be assessed for the actual product; this article does not prescribe certification, customs treatment or legal requirements.

Demand evidence for materials and workmanship

“Stainless steel” is not a complete material specification, and a polished photograph does not prove a grade. Ask the seller to mark product-contact parts, structural frames, heating surfaces, tanks, shafts, screw components and replaceable wear parts on a material schedule. Request stated grades, plate thicknesses, surface treatments, weld finishing and any available material certificates. Where material identity is commercially important, discuss independent verification before final acceptance.

Do not automatically require every frame and guard to be stainless steel. The important issue is whether materials are appropriate for their duty and whether product-contact, wear, corrosion and cleaning requirements have been considered. Ask how worn press components affect performance, how they are measured, and which parts should be stocked in Nigeria.

Use representative fruit for factory acceptance

A no-load motor run confirms rotation but does not establish processing capacity. Where practical, agree a factory acceptance test using representative palm fruit in the feed state specified in the contract. If equivalent fruit cannot be obtained at the manufacturing site, define what can be tested there and what must be proven during Nigerian site commissioning.

The test protocol should record feed identity and mass, starting and ending points, operating time, interruptions, operator count, transfer delays, power observations, leakage, vibration, control operation and discharge from each stage. Inspect fruit carry-over, press discharge, fibre-and-nut handling, crude liquid screening and movement into clarification. Capacity results remain supplier claims until tested under the agreed conditions.

Also verify guards, labels, emergency stops, cleaning access, manuals, wiring diagrams, spare-parts lists and machine serial details. Film the complete run without editing out stoppages. The checklist in What a Real China Factory Test Run for Machines Should Look Like applies whether the factory is overseas or local.

Questions to include in the RFQ

  1. Is this for mesocarp palm oil, palm kernel oil, or two separately defined lines?
  2. What exact material enters the first supplied machine: FFB, loose fruit, sterilised fruit, digested mash, nuts or kernels?
  3. Which processing stages are included, and which are expressly excluded?
  4. Is each capacity stated in FFB per hour, loose fruit per hour, mash per hour or oil output?
  5. For batch units, what are the working load and complete cycle time, including loading and discharge?
  6. What is the calculated bottleneck of the integrated line?
  7. What motors, controls, pumps, conveyors, pipework, platforms and tanks are included?
  8. What electrical, water, thermal and foundation requirements apply?
  9. Which parts contact fruit or oil, and what are their documented materials and thicknesses?
  10. Which wear parts and specialist tools are required for the first operating period?
  11. What test feed and acceptance procedure will be used?
  12. Who installs, commissions and troubleshoots the thermal, electrical and mechanical systems?
  13. What drawings, manuals, parts catalogues and maintenance schedules will be delivered?
  14. What is the warranty scope, and what costs or failure types are excluded?
  15. What arrangements exist for technical support and replacement parts in Nigeria?

A practical buying sequence

  1. Define the product and feed state.
  2. Document dependable monthly and peak FFB supply.
  3. Choose the required process boundary and optional kernel-recovery scope.
  4. Prepare the site utility and civil-work assumptions.
  5. Issue one RFQ format to shortlisted suppliers.
  6. Normalise quotations by included stages and FFB-equivalent capacity.
  7. Verify the supplier and inspect material evidence.
  8. Agree representative testing and written acceptance criteria.
  9. Calculate the installed project cost, spares and maintenance provision.
  10. Complete professional installation and commissioning before production.

If you need quotations compared on the same feed, process and capacity basis, submit a palm oil machine sourcing enquiry. Provide your location, available FFB, preferred throughput, power situation and the stages you expect the supplier to include.

Frequently asked questions

Can one oil expeller process both palm fruit and palm kernels?

Do not assume so. Mesocarp fruit pressing and kernel expelling use different prepared feeds. Require the manufacturer to identify the approved input, internal configuration and test evidence for the exact model.

What is the best capacity for a new Nigerian processor?

There is no universal best size. Base capacity on dependable seasonal FFB supply, operating hours, full batch cycles, transport condition, utilities, labour and the line's slowest station. Avoid sizing from hoped-for fruit purchases alone.

Does “one tonne per hour” always mean one tonne of FFB?

No. It may refer to FFB, loose fruit, digested mash, kernels or another feed. The quotation should state the material, moisture or preparation condition where relevant, and whether the rating is sustained or instantaneous.

Is kernel recovery part of a complete palm oil line?

Nut and kernel recovery may be included as an optional downstream section. Producing palm kernel oil requires further kernel preparation, expelling and filtration equipment, which should be treated as a separately defined line.

Can a press quotation be used as the total factory budget?

No. Include upstream preparation, clarification and storage, interconnections, civil works, power, water, thermal systems, waste handling, delivery, installation, commissioning, testing, spares and maintenance readiness.

Should the cheapest complete line win?

Only after quotations have been normalised. A lower offer may exclude a steriliser, clarification system, motors, controls, installation or testing. Compare the same feed state, process boundary, sustained capacity and support obligations before comparing price.
