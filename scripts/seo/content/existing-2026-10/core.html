Learning how to order from China to Nigeria involves more than finding a low unit price. You need to choose a suitable product, confirm that it can be imported, identify the legal supplier, document your specifications, control payment risk, plan quality checks and calculate the full cost of getting the goods to your location.

Importing is not automatically profitable, and no platform, supplier badge, payment method or procurement agent can remove every risk. A better approach is to make each stage verifiable before you commit more money.

This guide explains the process primarily for Nigerian importers. The supplier-verification, quotation, inspection and shipping principles also apply internationally, but readers outside Nigeria should check the customs, tax, product-safety and certification rules in their destination country.

How to Order From China to Nigeria: The Process at a Glance

  1. Choose a product and check demand, restrictions and regulatory requirements.
  2. Select a sourcing route such as Alibaba, 1688, Taobao, Tmall or a direct manufacturer.
  3. Prepare a detailed product specification and request comparable quotations.
  4. Verify the supplier's legal identity, capability and payment details.
  5. Order a sample or plan an inspection before approving bulk production.
  6. Agree on written order terms and use payment controls appropriate to the risk.
  7. Monitor production and inspect the goods where necessary.
  8. Choose air, sea or express shipping based on size, weight, urgency and product restrictions.
  9. Prepare Nigerian customs and product-compliance documents before shipment.
  10. Check the goods immediately after delivery and keep the transaction records.

Important Terms for First-Time Importers

  • MOQ: Minimum order quantity. This is the smallest quantity a supplier is willing to produce or sell under a particular quotation.
  • Landed cost: The total cost of getting the goods to the point where you can use or sell them. It can include the product, China-side transport, inspection, international freight, insurance, duties, taxes, terminal charges, clearance and local delivery.
  • CBM: Cubic metre. It measures cargo volume. One CBM is a space measuring one metre by one metre by one metre. Sea-freight quotations for consolidated cargo often depend partly on this measurement.
  • Volumetric weight: A calculated weight based on package dimensions rather than the reading on a scale. Air and express carriers may compare actual weight with volumetric weight and charge using the higher figure. The divisor can vary by carrier and service, so confirm the formula in your quotation. DHL's official guidance explains how dimensional weight affects express shipments.
  • Inspection: A structured check of the product, quantity, workmanship, packaging or performance against an agreed specification. It is more useful than receiving general photos from the supplier.
  • Procurement agent: A person or company that assists a buyer with tasks such as supplier communication, purchasing, consolidation, inspection or shipping. The exact responsibilities, fees and liability should be agreed in writing.

Step 1: Decide What to Order

Product selection affects demand, compliance, shipping cost and the amount of capital tied up in stock. Do not select an item only because its China price appears low.

Check demand before requesting a bulk order

  • Identify the likely buyer and the problem the product solves.
  • Compare the selling prices, specifications and reviews of similar products in Nigeria.
  • Estimate how quickly a realistic order quantity could sell.
  • Check whether customers require a warranty, installation, spare parts or after-sales support.
  • Consider exchange-rate exposure and the possibility that your selling price may change before the goods arrive.

Check whether the product can be imported

Before paying a supplier, confirm the product's description, intended use, material, composition and likely Harmonised System code, usually called an HS code. Nigerian importers can review current tariff classifications and prohibition information through the Nigeria Customs Service Tariff and CET portal and use the tools on the Nigeria Trade Portal.

Some goods are prohibited, restricted or regulated. Others may require a permit, product registration, conformity document or specialised shipping arrangement. Check this before ordering a sample if the product is sensitive.

Common product categories

Nigerian buyers commonly research categories such as:

  • phones, laptops and accessories;
  • consumer electronics and electrical products;
  • clothing, footwear and bags;
  • home, office and kitchen products;
  • toys and children's products;
  • beauty, food, supplement and other white-label products;
  • industrial tools, spare parts and production equipment; and
  • agricultural and food-processing machines.

This list is not a statement that every product in these categories is permitted, compliant or commercially attractive. Regulations and shipping restrictions apply to individual products, not just broad category names.

Step 2: Choose Where to Find the Product or Supplier

Different Chinese platforms serve different purchasing situations. A platform can help you discover sellers and process an order, but it does not automatically prove that a product is suitable for Nigeria or that a supplier can meet your specification.

  • Alibaba.com is an international business-to-business marketplace. It is useful for supplier enquiries, custom orders and export-oriented transactions. Where available, Alibaba's Trade Assurance may provide order-based dispute and payment protections, but the protection depends on the terms recorded in the online order and the buyer following the platform process. Review the official Trade Assurance guidance before paying.
  • 1688 is a Chinese-language wholesale marketplace. Prices and MOQs may differ from export-platform quotations, but buyers must confirm the seller's terms, domestic delivery arrangements, payment options, product specification and ability to provide export documents where needed.
  • Taobao and Tmall are retail-oriented marketplaces. They can be useful for ready-made products, samples and smaller quantities, but retail listings may not support custom manufacturing or export documentation.
  • Direct manufacturer directories and contact lists can provide additional leads. Treat any third-party list as a starting point, not as proof that a company is currently active, suitable or trustworthy.

Step 3: Prepare a Clear Product Specification

A supplier cannot quote or manufacture accurately if the request is vague. Write the specification before you focus on price.

Include details such as:

  • product name and intended use;
  • model, dimensions, weight and material;
  • colour, finish and acceptable tolerances;
  • electrical voltage, frequency and plug type where relevant;
  • performance, capacity or output requirements;
  • required components and accessories;
  • branding, label and packaging artwork;
  • carton quantity, carton dimensions and gross weight;
  • required test reports, registrations or conformity documents;
  • sample requirements;
  • inspection standard and acceptable defect limits;
  • quantity and desired delivery schedule; and
  • spare parts, manuals, installation or training requirements for machines.

Use photos, drawings or reference samples where possible, but state which features are mandatory. A photo alone may not communicate material grade, internal components, dimensions or performance.

Request quotations that can be compared

Ask each supplier to quote against the same specification and quantity. The quotation should identify:

  • unit price and currency;
  • MOQ;
  • sample price and sample-delivery terms;
  • tooling, mould, logo, packaging or setup charges;
  • production lead time;
  • China delivery cost and named delivery location;
  • carton size, quantity per carton, gross weight and total CBM;
  • payment schedule;
  • quotation validity;
  • what is included and excluded; and
  • the legal company issuing the quotation or proforma invoice.

Do not compare unit prices until you have checked whether the quotations describe the same product, packaging, quantity and delivery responsibility. For help reviewing quotations or invoices, use Supplier Intelligence.

Step 4: Verify the Supplier Before Paying

Supplier verification is not a single badge or document. It is the process of checking whether the company exists, whether the person contacting you represents it, whether it can supply the product and whether the order and payment details are consistent.

Check the legal entity

  • Request the supplier's Chinese business licence.
  • Record the full registered Chinese name and Unified Social Credit Code.
  • Search those details on China's National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System.
  • Check the registration status, address, business scope and any visible abnormal-operation notices.
  • Confirm which legal entity will sign the order, issue the invoice and receive payment.

A registered company can still deliver poor-quality goods, so registration is only one part of the check.

Check the supplier's capability

  • Ask whether the company is the manufacturer, a trading company or another intermediary.
  • Request a live video call from the stated office, warehouse or factory when the order justifies it.
  • Ask product-specific technical questions and compare the answers with the sample and quotation.
  • For custom goods, request evidence that the supplier has produced comparable items.
  • Where the risk is significant, arrange an independent factory audit, sample test or inspection.

Check certificates carefully

Do not accept a CE mark, ISO certificate, laboratory report, factory audit or SONCAP-related document merely because the supplier sent a PDF. Check the issuing body, certificate number, company name, product model, scope and validity. A genuine document may still be irrelevant to your exact product or destination.

Warning signs that require further investigation

  • a price that is far below comparable quotations without a clear explanation;
  • refusal to provide the registered Chinese company name;
  • conflicting names across the licence, invoice, email domain and bank beneficiary;
  • pressure to pay before the order specification is complete;
  • an unexplained last-minute change of bank account;
  • refusal to document promised specifications or remedies;
  • copied photos that cannot be supported with a live demonstration; or
  • requests to pay an unrelated person or company.

Poor English or the use of translation software is not, by itself, proof of fraud. Focus on verifiable identity, consistent documents, technical competence and willingness to put the agreement in writing.

Step 5: Negotiate Price, MOQ, Samples and Order Terms

Negotiation should improve the total deal, not simply force the lowest unit price. A supplier may reduce the price by changing the material, component, packaging, quality-control process or delivery responsibility.

Items you can discuss

  • unit price at different quantities;
  • MOQ and whether mixed colours or models can count towards it;
  • sample cost and whether it can be credited to a later bulk order;
  • packaging and labelling;
  • production schedule;
  • deposit and balance milestones;
  • inspection access;
  • spare parts or extra units;
  • remedies for shortages, defects or missed specifications; and
  • China-side delivery to your warehouse or freight forwarder.

No language, agent or negotiation tactic guarantees a saving. Compare several technically equivalent quotations and judge the final landed cost, risk and supplier capability together.

When to order a sample

A sample is particularly useful for products where feel, finish, fit, colour, packaging or user experience matters. It is also useful for electronics, accessories, beauty packaging and custom-branded goods.

Compare the sample with a written checklist. If it is approved, keep it as the reference or “golden sample” for bulk production. A good sample does not guarantee that the bulk order will match it, which is why production controls and inspection may still be necessary.

For large machines that cannot reasonably be sent as samples, request a live or recorded test using an agreed test procedure. Consider an on-site inspection before shipment.

Step 6: Use Risk-Based Payment Controls

There are not only four safe payment methods, and no method guarantees supplier performance or recovery. Payment risk depends on the supplier, the order contract, the platform or bank process, the amount already paid, the evidence available and the buyer's ability to stop or dispute the transaction.

Common payment structures

  • Platform payment with order protection: Where available, keep the product specification, shipment deadline, quality terms and payment inside the protected platform order. Alibaba states that Trade Assurance protection is based on the online order and payment through its approved process. Read the official guide and current terms before relying on it.
  • Corporate bank transfer: A transfer to a business account may be appropriate for an independently verified supplier, but it can be difficult to reverse. Verify the contracting entity, invoice, beneficiary, bank country and reason for any mismatch before payment.
  • RMB payment through a procurement agent: This can be useful for domestic Chinese marketplaces or suppliers, but the buyer should know who receives the funds, what checks the agent performs, the fees, the exchange rate, what documents will be issued and what happens if the order fails.
  • Escrow or card-based payment: These may provide additional transaction controls in some situations, but coverage, deadlines, exclusions and evidence requirements vary.

Controls to apply before releasing money

  • Verify the supplier's legal name independently.
  • Check that the proforma invoice matches the agreed specification, quantity, currency and payment schedule.
  • Confirm who owns the beneficiary account and document any legitimate third-party arrangement.
  • Do not treat a similar account name as proof that the payment is safe.
  • Verify every account or payment-procedure change through a second channel using contact details you already trust. The FBI's business email compromise guidance specifically recommends independently checking account changes.
  • For protected marketplace orders, keep negotiations and evidence on the platform.
  • Link payment milestones to measurable events where the supplier accepts that structure.
  • Use two-person approval for significant payments if you are buying for a business.
  • Keep invoices, contracts, messages, bank confirmations, inspection reports and shipping records.

Be cautious with payments to personal accounts, unrelated businesses, unknown intermediaries or irreversible channels. The important question is not only which method is being used, but also who is being paid, under what documented order and with what practical recovery route.

Step 7: Monitor Production and Inspect the Goods

Once production begins, confirm that the supplier is working from the final approved specification rather than an earlier chat message or sample version.

During production

  • confirm approved artwork, dimensions, colours and packaging;
  • request dated production updates at agreed milestones;
  • check whether materials or components have changed;
  • confirm the expected completion date;
  • send the inspection checklist before production is completed; and
  • do not approve shipment merely because cartons are visible in a photo.

What an inspection can check

  • quantity and model mix;
  • appearance and workmanship;
  • dimensions, weight and material where testable;
  • basic function and performance;
  • labels, barcodes, manuals and packaging;
  • carton dimensions, gross weight and shipping marks; and
  • comparison with the approved sample or specification.

Inspection reduces uncertainty but does not guarantee that every hidden defect will be found. Define the sample size, tests, defect categories and pass-or-fail rules before the inspection.

Extra checks for machines and production equipment

  • Confirm the required output using the buyer's actual or representative raw material where possible.
  • Record the test method, test duration, output, waste rate and finished-product quality.
  • Verify voltage, frequency, phase, motor specifications and utility requirements.
  • Check the machine's dimensions, gross weight, packing method and lifting points.
  • List all included moulds, tools, accessories and spare parts.
  • Request operation, maintenance and wiring documents in a usable language.
  • Clarify installation, commissioning, remote support and operator-training arrangements.
  • Check product-specific safety and destination-country compliance requirements.

A CE document, ISO certificate or factory report should not be treated as universally required or sufficient. The correct evidence depends on the machine, its components, intended use and destination. Use LineScout when researching machines or production lines, and use Supplier Intelligence to review the supplier, quotation or invoice.

Step 8: Choose the Shipping Method

Your shipping method should reflect the product, package dimensions, actual weight, urgency, value, handling risk and regulatory status. Do not rely on a generic China-to-Nigeria delivery promise without knowing what the quoted time includes.

Express courier

Express services can suit samples, documents and small time-sensitive parcels. The quote may depend on the higher of actual or volumetric weight. Confirm whether duties, taxes, remote-area charges and destination delivery are included.

Air freight

Air freight can suit urgent or higher-value commercial cargo where the margin can absorb the freight cost. Ask whether the quote is airport-to-airport, warehouse-to-airport or door-to-door. Confirm chargeable weight, handling charges, customs arrangements and the estimated schedule for the exact route.

Sea freight

Sea freight can suit machines, heavy goods and larger commercial orders. Consolidated or less-than-container-load cargo may be quoted by volume or another chargeable measurement, while full-container shipments involve container and destination charges. Ask for the sailing schedule, origin charges, destination charges, free-storage period and what happens if the cargo misses the planned vessel.

Why fixed shipping-time claims can mislead

“Transit time” may cover only the main flight or ocean voyage. Total delivery time can also include supplier dispatch, China domestic transport, warehouse receiving, consolidation, export handling, booking delays, trans-shipment, customs processing and local delivery. Request a current route-specific estimate and ask the provider to separate these stages.

Special warning for batteries and sensitive cargo

Phones, laptops, power banks and many other products contain lithium batteries. IATA classifies lithium batteries as dangerous goods for air transport and applies product, testing, packaging, marking and documentation requirements. Confirm acceptance with a competent carrier or freight forwarder before purchase and review the current IATA Battery Shipping Regulations information.

Liquids, powders, magnets, chemicals, aerosols, food, cosmetics and oversized equipment may also require special handling or carrier approval. Tell the freight provider exactly what the goods are rather than using a vague description.

Step 9: Plan Nigerian Customs and Product Compliance

Nigeria-specific import requirements should be checked before shipment, not after the goods arrive. Using a freight forwarder or clearing agent can reduce your administrative workload, but the importer should still understand the declaration, product description, supporting documents and charges.

Start with the correct product description and HS code

The HS code affects tariff classification, possible restrictions and regulatory treatment. Use the Nigeria Customs Service CET portal and the Nigeria Trade Portal tariff search. Where classification is uncertain or financially significant, obtain professional advice or an official ruling rather than choosing the code with the lowest apparent rate.

Prepare the import documents early

Confirm the documentation checklist for your exact product and shipping arrangement before placing the order. Use the Nigeria Customs Service website and helpdesk to seek current guidance, and agree with your freight or customs professional which documents are needed, who prepares them and when they must be ready.

Depending on the shipment, you may need:

  • proforma invoice;
  • Form M;
  • insurance evidence;
  • product or import permits;
  • commercial invoice;
  • packing list;
  • bill of lading, airway bill or waybill;
  • certificate of origin;
  • SONCAP or another conformity document where applicable;
  • NAFDAC registration, permit or clearance documents where applicable; and
  • other product-specific approvals.

Understand SON and SONCAP

The Standards Organisation of Nigeria, or SON, administers standards and conformity processes. SON describes SONCAP as a pre-shipment conformity assessment process for products regulated under the programme. Not every imported product follows the same route, and exemptions or import permits may apply in specific circumstances. Check the current official SONCAP guidance before the goods are shipped.

Understand NAFDAC

The National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control, or NAFDAC, regulates categories that include foods, drugs, cosmetics, medical devices and chemicals. Requirements differ by product and purpose. Use the official NAFDAC services portal and its product-specific regulatory guidelines rather than relying solely on a supplier's certificate.

Estimate duties and destination charges

Do not use a single assumed customs percentage for every product. Duties, taxes and other import charges depend on factors including classification, customs value, applicable trade rules and the nature of the shipment. Confirm what your freight or clearance quotation includes and what will be billed separately.

Readers importing into another country should replace the Nigeria-specific checks above with the official customs, standards, food-and-drug, product-safety and tax requirements of their destination.

Step 10: Calculate the Landed Cost Before Approving the Order

The lowest factory price may not produce the lowest final cost. Build a landed-cost estimate before paying the deposit.

A practical estimate may include:

  • product and packaging cost;
  • sample, tooling, mould or artwork charges;
  • China domestic delivery;
  • supplier-verification or procurement fees;
  • inspection and laboratory testing;
  • export handling and documentation;
  • international freight;
  • cargo insurance;
  • duties, taxes and regulatory charges;
  • port, terminal, storage and handling charges;
  • clearing-agent fees;
  • local delivery;
  • bank, platform, payment and currency-conversion costs; and
  • the cost of defects, spare parts, warranty support or unsold stock.

Divide the total by the number of saleable units, not merely the number ordered. If some units are reserved for testing, replacements or likely defects, account for that in your unit economics without assuming a guaranteed defect rate.

Ask for written freight and clearance quotations that clearly state their assumptions, validity period, package dimensions, weight, delivery point and exclusions.

Step 11: Receive and Check the Shipment

Delivery is not the end of the ordering process. Inspect the shipment promptly while records and any platform dispute window are still available.

  • Count cartons and note visible damage before accepting delivery where possible.
  • Photograph the external packaging, labels and damaged areas.
  • Compare models, colours and quantities with the packing list.
  • Test a reasonable sample immediately.
  • Keep packaging and shipping labels until the checks are complete.
  • Report shortages or damage through the correct platform, insurer, carrier or supplier channel within the applicable deadline.
  • Record actual landed cost and delivery time to improve the next order.

Delivery times within Nigeria depend on the arrival point, clearance status, final destination, carrier and local conditions. Obtain a shipment-specific estimate rather than assuming a fixed Lagos or interstate delivery period.

When to Use a China Procurement Agent

A procurement agent can be useful when the transaction involves language barriers, domestic Chinese platforms, several suppliers, custom production, complex equipment, inspections or consolidation. The agent should have a defined scope rather than a vague promise to handle everything.

Before appointing an agent, ask:

  • Which legal entity are you contracting with?
  • What services and product categories are covered?
  • How are fees and exchange rates calculated?
  • Will supplier identities and original quotations be disclosed?
  • Who receives the buyer's funds and who pays the supplier?
  • What verification or inspection is actually included?
  • Who is responsible for errors in the specification or order?
  • Which shipping, customs and destination charges are excluded?
  • What records will the buyer receive?
  • How are disputes, refunds and damaged goods handled?

An agent can reduce workload and help coordinate checks, but cannot guarantee supplier performance, customs outcomes, shipping schedules or profit.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Choosing a product before checking restrictions: A good selling idea is not useful if the item cannot be legally or economically imported.
  2. Trusting a platform profile without verifying the legal entity: Platform status is not a substitute for company and order checks.
  3. Requesting only the lowest price: The supplier may quote a different specification, packaging standard or delivery responsibility.
  4. Paying from a chat message instead of a complete invoice or protected order: This weakens your evidence if a dispute occurs.
  5. Ignoring a bank-account change: Verify every change independently before sending funds.
  6. Approving bulk production from photos alone: Use samples, measurable specifications and inspection where the risk justifies it.
  7. Assuming every certificate is required or sufficient: Check the exact product and destination rule.
  8. Using the scale weight alone for air cargo: Bulky goods may be billed by volumetric weight.
  9. Confusing transit time with door-to-door time: Consolidation, customs and local delivery can add separate stages.
  10. Calculating profit from product price alone: Use landed cost and realistic saleable quantity.
  11. Buying a machine without an agreed test procedure: Appearance does not prove capacity, compatibility or finished-product quality.
  12. Shipping before documents are ready: Missing or inconsistent paperwork can lead to delays and extra charges.

Keep one order record from quotation to delivery

When you import from China to Nigeria, several people may handle the same order: the supplier, purchasing agent, inspector, warehouse and freight provider. Give the order a reference and use it consistently. Record who approves each handoff so a warehouse receipt does not accidentally become approval of product quality or permission to ship.

Handoff Record to keep Decision before moving on
Enquiry to quotation Product links, model, material, variants, quantities and destination. Are all suppliers quoting the same order?
Quotation to payment Final invoice, verified seller and beneficiary, payment terms and agreed inclusions. Are unexplained differences resolved in writing?
Production to acceptance Approved sample, inspection scope, findings and recorded corrective actions. Does the buyer accept the findings and any remaining exceptions?
Warehouse to shipment Carton count, packed dimensions, gross weight, shipment quote and document checklist. Do the goods, costs and documents match the agreed shipment?
Delivery to claim or closure Receiving count, photographs, defects and the agreed claims procedure. Are shortages or damage recorded promptly and sent to the responsible party?

For a multi-supplier order, keep a separate line for each seller and parcel. Record ordered quantity, quantity received at the warehouse, discrepancies and approval status. Otherwise an apparently complete shipment can hide a missing parcel or incorrect variant. Ask who will resolve a discrepancy before consolidation and what evidence they need from you.

What to do when the order changes

If the supplier proposes a substitute material, a different model or a revised dispatch date, request a revised written confirmation. Check the effect on compatibility, inspection, freight and your own customer commitments before accepting. A change agreed in a chat should also appear in the final order record, particularly when the invoice still describes the original specification.

Keep cost changes separate from technical changes. A cheaper unit can become a more expensive delivered order if the packaging grows or additional equipment is required. Ask for the final packed information and update the estimate before approving shipment. If an assumption remains unresolved, label it as an estimate rather than turning it into a fixed promise to a customer.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I order products from China to Nigeria?

Start by defining the product and specifications, checking whether Nigerian import or certification requirements apply, comparing suppliers and quotations, reviewing payment risk, and planning inspection, shipping and clearance before placing the order. Buyers outside Nigeria can follow the same process while checking their own local rules.

Can I order from Chinese websites and deliver to Nigeria?

Yes, but the purchasing and delivery process depends on the website, seller and product. If you already have a product link from 1688, Taobao, Tmall or another Chinese website, a buying service can help purchase the item and arrange the next steps.

How can I check a Chinese supplier before paying?

Verify the supplier’s registered business details, account name, product capability, quotation and supporting documents. Request samples or appropriate inspection evidence where practical, and investigate unexplained payment-detail changes. No single badge, document or payment method removes every risk.

What costs should I consider before ordering from China?

Build a landed-cost estimate covering the product, packaging, inspection, local transport in China, freight, insurance where applicable, clearance, duties or taxes, certification and delivery. Obtain current quotations and confirm Nigerian requirements with authoritative sources because costs and rules can change.

Do products imported into Nigeria require certification?

Requirements depend on the product category and intended use. Check current guidance from relevant Nigerian authorities, such as the Nigeria Customs Service, Standards Organisation of Nigeria or NAFDAC, where applicable. Non-Nigerian readers should consult the corresponding authorities in their destination country.
