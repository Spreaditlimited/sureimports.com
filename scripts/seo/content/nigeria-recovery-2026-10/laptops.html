Choose the laptop around the work it must do

Write down the applications you actually use, including any browser-heavy workflow, video meetings, spreadsheets, design software or specialist business package. Then check the software publisher's current requirements. The manufacturer's minimum requirement is a compatibility floor; it is not a promise of comfortable performance with several applications open at once.

For a student or general office buyer, keyboard comfort, readable display, portability, a functioning webcam and dependable battery may matter more than an impressive processor label. For design, engineering or video work, check the particular application's graphics, memory and display requirements before selecting a model. A laptop that is suitable for accounting work may be unsuitable for rendering or demanding games even if both adverts say “Core i7”.

For programmers, consider the tools, local databases, virtual machines and development environments you expect to run. For a sales team, test the actual business software, video-call setup and external display arrangement. Ask whether RAM and storage can be upgraded on the precise model; some configurations have soldered memory or different internal layouts. Do not budget for an upgrade until compatibility and labour cost are confirmed.

Use a short purchasing brief: intended user, applications, minimum acceptable specification, preferred screen size, condition, battery acceptance criteria, quantity, delivery deadline and total budget. Keep optional preferences separate from requirements. This helps a supplier suggest alternatives without quietly downgrading something essential.

Understand new, used and refurbished laptop descriptions

“Used”, “refurbished”, “open box” and “Grade A” do not describe one universal condition. A used machine may have received only basic functional checks. A refurbished machine may have had components replaced, but the scope of that work depends on who did it. An attractive casing does not establish battery condition, repair history or remaining component life.

Ask the seller to define the grade in writing. The definition should cover visible marks, screen defects, keyboard wear, hinges, missing feet, ports, charger condition and battery acceptance. If a supplier permits scratches but excludes cracked housings, record that distinction. For a mixed batch, establish whether every unit meets the same criteria and how exceptions will be reported.

Manufacturer refurbishment and independent seller refurbishment also carry different support arrangements. Confirm who stands behind the warranty, where service is available and whether the actual serial number qualifies. A warranty offered by a supplier in China is not automatically a locally serviceable manufacturer warranty in Nigeria.

Check the full specification and operating-system compatibility

Record the exact processor model rather than just the processor family. Confirm installed memory, storage capacity and type, screen resolution, graphics hardware, keyboard layout and available ports. If the advert mentions a touchscreen, backlit keyboard or fingerprint reader, include that feature in the purchase specification and inspection checklist.

A machine advertised with Windows installed is not necessarily eligible for the supported configuration you need. Microsoft's Windows 11 requirements include processor compatibility, TPM 2.0 and appropriate firmware features. Check the actual device against the current requirements rather than relying on a screenshot of a Windows desktop. Also confirm the licence arrangements for Windows and any paid applications.

Check that firmware settings can be accessed appropriately and that the device is not tied to an organisation's management or activation controls. Ask the supplier to demonstrate a clean setup suitable for the next owner. This is especially important for former corporate stock: a laptop that boots during a short inspection can still present management restrictions when it is reset or connected later.

Battery condition needs evidence, not a vague promise

Ask for a battery report or the manufacturer's diagnostic result tied to the inspected unit. Agree how battery condition will be assessed and what outcome is acceptable before purchase. A quoted runtime without a workload, screen brightness or test procedure is difficult to compare. A short idle test is not the same as a video meeting or a demanding application session.

For a batch, record the findings against serial numbers. Confirm whether the same battery acceptance threshold applies to every unit, whether a replacement is included for failures and who pays for that replacement. If the supplier cannot provide dependable battery evidence, include the uncertainty in your buying decision instead of advertising an unsupported runtime to customers.

The charger also belongs in the specification. Confirm the compatible charger rating, connector and supplied lead, and test that the laptop charges normally during use. A cheap substitute can change the value of an otherwise attractive purchase. Ask about charger authenticity and condition rather than assuming every included accessory is the original one.

Run a practical pre-shipment inspection

An inspection brief should explain what will be checked, which units will be checked and what happens when a failure is found. For a small, valuable batch, individual testing may be worth pricing. For a larger order, discuss the sampling plan and its limitations. A sample pass cannot prove that every untested laptop is fault-free.

  • Match model and serial details to the approved order.
  • Inspect screen condition, hinges, casing and keyboard layout.
  • Test keys, trackpad, webcam, microphone, speakers and headphone output.
  • Check the required USB, display, charging and network connections.
  • Verify memory, storage and processor details on the actual unit.
  • Review battery evidence, charging behaviour and agreed functional tests.
  • Confirm the operating-system setup and absence of unresolved management restrictions.
  • Record the charger, accessories, packing and any accepted cosmetic defects.

Ask for legible photographs and test records that identify the inspected units. An unrelated promotional video is weak evidence for your order. If the inspection finds missing accessories, damaged screens or substituted specifications, resolve those exceptions before authorising shipment. Keep the approved report with the invoice and packing list for later receiving checks.

Calculate the landed cost of importing laptops

The supplier's unit price is only one line in the budget. Add the payment conversion and transaction costs, inspection, local movement in China, packing, international freight, applicable clearance charges and delivery to your destination. Confirm what each service quotation includes so you do not omit a cost or count it twice.

The following is a hypothetical arithmetic example, not a quotation or a statement of current duty rates. Suppose a batch of ten laptops costs ₦3,000,000 after currency conversion. Suppose inspection and China-side handling total ₦120,000, the quoted freight and clearance provision totals ₦500,000, and local delivery costs ₦30,000. The example reaches ₦3,650,000, or ₦365,000 per unit, before any additional business overhead, repairs or warranty provision.

If one laptop then needs an unplanned ₦50,000 repair, the batch economics change. If your customers require bags, software setup or delivery, those costs also matter. Work through the same calculation using actual written quotations before comparing the imported unit with a local seller's ready-to-use offer. Our landed-cost estimator helps organise the arithmetic; confirm the charge inputs for your shipment separately.

For resale, calculate contribution after marketplace charges, payment fees, customer delivery and expected support costs. A difference between buying price and selling price is not automatically profit. For internal business use, include setup time, failure handling and the cost of staff waiting for a usable replacement.

When buying locally may be the better choice

For one urgently needed laptop, a local purchase may be more practical if you can inspect it, confirm the specification and understand the returns process. The ability to resolve a problem nearby has value. Compare that value with the full cost and time of importing, rather than assuming that China sourcing must be cheaper for every order.

Importing becomes more interesting when you have a clear batch requirement, enough time to inspect and ship, and a supplier able to meet a consistent specification. Even then, compare a local batch quotation. If the difference is small, better local service or faster availability may outweigh a lower supplier price abroad.

A mixed batch offered at a discount can be difficult to resell or support. Ten laptops with different processors, batteries, keyboards and chargers are ten separate customer explanations. Decide whether that complexity fits your business. For a school or office deployment, uniformity may be worth more than the lowest blended purchase price.

Shipping, receiving and support arrangements

Laptops contain batteries and need an accepted shipping route. Provide the exact goods, battery condition and quantity to the freight provider before committing to purchase. Confirm packaging and carrier requirements directly; do not assume that every route accepts every battery-containing shipment. Discuss available cover, exclusions and the procedure for reporting transit damage.

Protect screens and corners, secure accessories and label cartons so receiving staff can reconcile the delivery with the packing list. Keep serial-number records. When the goods arrive, inspect packaging, count the units and repeat the essential functional checks promptly. Record exceptions with photographs and clear descriptions within the applicable claim period.

For ongoing business purchases, keep a simple asset register with model, serial number, user, delivery date, supplier and support contact. Separate the supplier's warranty from any additional promise you make to your own customers. Never advertise a replacement period or free repair service unless the terms and operational capacity actually support it.

Questions Nigerian laptop buyers should ask

Is a higher processor number always better value?

No single family label settles the decision. Compare the exact processor model, intended software, memory, storage, cooling, condition and support. A newer, suitable configuration may be a better purchase than an older machine carrying a more impressive family name.

Can I rely on a seller's Grade A description?

Only after you understand its written definition and inspection evidence. Ask what defects are permitted, how batteries are assessed, which accessories are included and what happens when a delivered unit fails the agreed standard.

Should I import one laptop or a batch?

Compare actual local and delivered import quotations. A single urgent purchase often benefits from local inspection and support. A well-specified batch may justify sourcing and inspection work, but quantity alone does not guarantee savings or quality.

What should I send before requesting a quotation?

Send the use case, preferred model or minimum specification, acceptable condition, quantity, location, deadline and total budget. Include any non-negotiable requirement such as keyboard layout, battery acceptance or a particular application. That information supports a meaningful quotation instead of a misleading headline price.
