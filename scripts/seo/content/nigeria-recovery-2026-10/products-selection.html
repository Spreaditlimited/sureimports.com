Validate demand before calling a product a best seller

Record actual buying signals: recent customer requests, comparable products sold by nearby retailers, recurring complaints and the prices buyers accept. Likes, views and a supplier's “hot sale” label are weaker evidence than a customer prepared to purchase at a price that covers your costs. Ask potential buyers what they currently use, what fails and what they would change.

Compare like-for-like listings. A cheap rechargeable fan with an unspecified battery is not necessarily the same product as a tested model with clear runtime conditions. A laptop's processor generation and condition affect its market. A kitchen product's dimensions can matter as much as its appearance. Save the specifications alongside the price so your research does not collapse unlike products into one misleading average.

Use a sample or a carefully sized trial order where practical. Define what you are testing: durability, customer acceptance, packing damage, repeat demand or willingness to pay. Write down a decision before the trial ends, such as rejecting a supplier if the sample fails an agreed functional check. Do not use a small trial's result as a guarantee that every future batch will perform identically.

Check profit after delivery, returns and selling costs

A hypothetical example shows the difference between a price gap and a useful margin. Suppose a product costs ₦3,000 to buy and a further ₦1,000 per unit to land. At a ₦6,000 selling price, the initial difference is ₦2,000. If packaging, payment charges, delivery support and an allowance for returns together cost ₦800 per unit, the remaining contribution is ₦1,200 before your other overheads. These numbers demonstrate the calculation; they are not current quotations for any product in this list.

Now test what happens if the selling price falls, freight increases or part of the batch remains unsold. A business that makes money only when every assumption is favourable needs a smaller test or a different product. Match technical products to the support you can provide

Machines, laptops and power equipment can attract valuable enquiries, but they need more than attractive photographs. If you cannot explain the specification, inspect the goods or handle an after-sales question, arrange suitable support before selling them.
